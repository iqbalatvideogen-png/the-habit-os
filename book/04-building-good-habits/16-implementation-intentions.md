## What Are Implementation Intentions

Implementation intentions are if-then plans that specify exactly when, where, and how you will perform a behavior.

Format: If situation, then I will behavior.

Developed by Peter Gollwitzer in the 1990s. Research shows they double or triple the likelihood of following through on intentions.

---

## The Science Behind Implementation Intentions

When you create an implementation intention, your brain creates a strong mental link between the situation and the behavior. When the situation occurs, your brain automatically retrieves the planned behavior.

This reduces decision fatigue and increases follow-through.

The prefrontal cortex creates the plan, the basal ganglia executes it automatically, and the hippocampus stores the context.

---

## The Implementation Intention Formula

Basic Structure: If situation, then I will behavior.

Components:
- Situation: The specific cue or trigger (time, location, event, emotion)
- Behavior: The specific action you will take (what, how, how long, how much)

Examples:
- If it is 7 AM, then I will go for a 30-minute walk.
- If I sit at my desk, then I will write my to-do list.
- If I feel stressed, then I will take 3 deep breaths.

---

## Creating Effective Implementation Intentions

Step 1: Identify Your Goal - Be specific about the outcome

Step 2: Identify the Situation - Choose a specific, predictable situation

Step 3: Specify the Behavior - Be very specific about what you will do

Step 4: Make It Realistic - Start small and build up gradually

Step 5: Write It Down - Put it in writing and post it where you will see it

Step 6: Test and Refine - Try it out and adjust as needed

---

## Types of Implementation Intentions

1. Time-Based: If it is specific time, then I will behavior.

2. Location-Based: If I am in specific location, then I will behavior.

3. Event-Based: If specific event happens, then I will behavior.

4. Emotion-Based: If I feel specific emotion, then I will behavior.

5. Social-Based: If specific person does specific action, then I will behavior.

---

## Implementation Intentions for Habit Formation

Use implementation intentions to build new habits by specifying exactly when and how you will perform the behavior.

Example for Meditation: If I finish brushing my teeth, then I will meditate for 5 minutes using my meditation app.

Use the inversion technique to break bad habits: If I feel stressed, then I will take a 5-minute walk instead of eating junk food.

---

## Advanced Strategies

1. Chain Implementation Intentions - Link multiple intentions together

2. Contingency Implementation Intentions - Plan for obstacles: If obstacle, then I will solution.

3. Combine with Habit Stacking - Use both strategies together

4. Combine with Environment Design - Create supportive surroundings

---

## Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Vague Situations - Make the situation specific and predictable

Mistake 2: Vague Behaviors - Make the behavior specific and actionable

Mistake 3: Unrealistic Intentions - Start small and build up gradually

Mistake 4: Too Many Intentions - Start with 1-2 and build from there

Mistake 5: Not Writing Them Down - Write them down and post them

Mistake 6: Not Reviewing - Review regularly and track progress

## Summary

Implementation intentions are if-then plans that specify exactly when, where, and how you will perform a behavior. They work by creating strong mental links, reducing decision fatigue, and increasing follow-through. Use them to build new habits, break bad habits, and overcome obstacles. Advanced strategies include chaining, contingency planning, and combining with other habit strategies.
