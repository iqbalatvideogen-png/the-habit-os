## Research Insights

### The Neuroscience of Vision

The reticular activating system (RAS) acts as a filter, determining what information we notice (Magoun, 1952). The prefrontal cortex handles goal setting, while the hippocampus creates mental simulations of future scenarios (Schacter et al., 2012).

### Vision and Motivation

Self-Determination Theory identifies autonomy, competence, and relatedness as core psychological needs (Deci & Ryan, 1985). A compelling vision satisfies all three, providing direction and fueling motivation through dopamine release.

### Goal Setting Theory

Locke and Lathams research shows that specific, challenging goals increase performance by 10-25% over vague goals (2002). Written goals are 42% more likely to be achieved (Matthews, 2015).

## Common Mistakes

1. Vague Vision - Lack of clarity leads to lack of direction
2. Vision Without Action - Having a vision but not taking steps
3. Inauthentic Vision - Pursuing others expectations
4. Static Vision - Never revisiting or updating
5. Vision Without Why - Goals without deeper purpose

---

## Myths vs Evidence

### Myth: Vision is only for leaders
Reality: Everyone benefits from a clear vision (Harter et al., 2002)

### Myth: I dont need to write my vision down
Reality: Written visions are 42% more likely to be achieved (Matthews, 2015)

### Myth: Vision limits creativity
Reality: Vision enhances creativity by providing direction (Stroud, 2008)
